\chapter{Evaluation and Discussion}
\label{ch:evaluation}

\section{Central Empirical Finding}

The strongest result from the current corpus is not a detection F1 score. It is the systematic mismatch between benign folder labels and rule-based triage signals.

In \texttt{extracted\_data/dataset\_manifest.csv} ($n{=}30$), \textbf{all 15} benign-labelled (\texttt{NoVirus}) rows are marked \texttt{uncertain=True}. Reasons include HIGH or CRITICAL verdicts, elevated process scores, injection counts, and C2-style connection counts despite benign labels; even the W32.MyDoom.A.-NoVirus row is flagged \texttt{ambiguous\_novirus\_control} despite LOW triage verdicts. Malware-labelled rows are not flagged uncertain in the manifest.

Figure~\ref{fig:dataset_overview} summarises label balance and the uncertain-benign split. This pattern means folder naming is an imperfect proxy for ground truth: ``clean'' baselines in memory forensics can still contain suspicious artefacts from security tools, prior activity, or capture timing.

For machine learning, uncertain benign rows should be excluded, down-weighted, or reported separately in evaluation. Treating all NoVirus folders as definitively benign will inflate false conclusions about separability.

\section{Proof-of-Concept Scope}

This chapter evaluates whether the pipeline produces consistent artefacts, meaningful graphs, and auditable evidence on a small research corpus. It does \textbf{not} claim production malware detection performance. The main quantitative ML results are Tracks~2 and~3 (binary GNN and two-model fusion) on the expanded \texttt{extracted\_csvs} manifest ($n{=}43$).

Two manifest views are used. The \texttt{extracted\_data} corpus ($n{=}30$) supports descriptive statistics, uncertainty figures, and family case studies in \S\ref{sec:descriptive_corpus}--\S\ref{sec:failure_cases}. The \texttt{extracted\_csvs} corpus ($n{=}43$) is the primary evaluation set for trained checkpoints, calibration, and abstention (Tables in \S\ref{sec:quant_ml_eval}). Benign-labelled rows in both corpora remain dominated by \texttt{ambiguous\_novirus\_control}; they stress-test false-positive pressure but are not a clean enterprise benign baseline.

\section{Failure Cases and Edge Families}
\label{sec:failure_cases}

\subsection{W32.MyDoom.A. pair (very small graphs)}

W32.MyDoom.A.-NoVirus (86 nodes) and W32.MyDoom.A.-WithVirus (164 nodes) are outliers. Both report LOW verdicts and zero injections; the benign row is still \texttt{uncertain=True} as an \texttt{ambiguous\_novirus\_control}. Models trained mainly on large graphs may underfit or overfit on tiny topologies---pooling and normalisation should be checked on this pair.

\subsection{Cerber pair (similar scale, divergent C2 counts)}

Cerber-WithVirus (7,904 nodes) and Cerber-NoVirus (9,382 nodes) have comparable scale. The malware row reports 24 C2 connections and \texttt{lolbin\_c2\_found=True}; the benign row reports 7 C2 connections and is uncertain. Both share HIGH verdicts and six injections in the manifest. A GNN may not separate these folders if features emphasise shared injection structure more than family-specific infection cues.

\subsection{GoldenEye pair (high injection on both rows)}

GoldenEye-WithVirus has the highest injection count (41). GoldenEye-NoVirus still reports 8 injections, 13 hidden processes, and a CRITICAL verdict. This is among the clearest uncertain-benign examples: triage treats both snapshots as highly suspicious.

\subsection{When a GNN may not help}

GNNs add value when relational structure carries signal beyond graph-level heuristics. They may add little when: (1) labels are dominated by triage noise; (2) graphs differ mainly in size, not infection topology; (3) train and test families are too few for stable grouped metrics; or (4) edge and node features already encode the decision rules. The uncertain-benign prevalence pushes toward analyst-review workflows and two-model conformity scores rather than hard binary automation.

\section{Binary and Two-Model Discussion}

The binary path assigns calibrated malware probability and four triage bands \cite{guo2017calibration}. On the latest 43-row run, all samples received \texttt{likely\_malicious} at very high calibrated probability---a saturated pattern that reflects both label--signal mismatch and coupling between rule-derived graph attributes and the classifier. Hold-out validation metrics (Table~\ref{tab:binary_train_meta}) remain modest (AUROC near chance on the strict split), so decisive F1 against folder labels is not a reliable success criterion.

The two-model path fuses binary probability, malware/benign conformity scores, and heuristic risk \cite{ruff2018deep}. With \texttt{abstention\_mode=calibrated}, it routes most rows to \texttt{needs\_analyst\_review} (review rate ${\approx}0.98$, decisive coverage ${\approx}0$). That behaviour matches the design goal on this corpus: surface ambiguity instead of forcing a binary verdict when dual scores overlap.

These paths are \textbf{inference evaluations} on trained checkpoints (hold-out validation for binary training; one-class spheres for malware/benign). They are reported separately from the exploratory grouped CV in Track~1.

\subsection{Forensic diagnosis of the two-model run}

The 43-row evaluation made the score geometry explicit. Malware and benign one-class conformity scores often occupy overlapping bands, so the dual-model margin is small. The fusion layer therefore abstains rather than issuing decisive \texttt{likely\_malicious} or \texttt{likely\_benign} states.

This is not best interpreted as a single implementation bug. It reflects three connected issues: rule-derived features can leak triage decisions into the binary model; the benign one-class model lacks a clean benign manifold when trained on ambiguous \texttt{NoVirus} controls; and fixed OR-style abstention gates can saturate review routing when score geometry is poorly separated.

The redesigned evaluation scripts therefore report review-routing rate, decisive coverage, gate-trigger counts, and score percentiles. These quantities are more informative than accuracy alone for this corpus because many rows are not cleanly separable by label.

\subsection{Redesign ablations and remaining data gap}

The final codebase supports several ablations that directly target the diagnosis. The \texttt{no\_manifest\_leakage} graph-attribute profile masks rule-derived manifest fields during training and inference. The calibrated abstention mode reduces reliance on binary--dual disagreement when the binary probability is already saturated. The gate-ablation path in \texttt{evaluate.py} compares disabled, legacy, and calibrated uncertainty routing. The \texttt{attack\_subgraph} view supports experiments on causal subgraphs rather than full memory-heavy graphs.

These ablations are implemented as evaluation support. Their scientific value depends on data quality. In strict mode, both binary training and benign one-class training require train-eligible benign rows with \texttt{clean\_benign} or explicitly included hard-benign subtype labels. Without those rows, the only immediate option is to train experimentally on \texttt{ambiguous\_novirus\_control} rows, which should be reported as a stress test rather than as a clean benign baseline.

\section{Explainability}

The project records top graph attributes, important nodes, and edge pairs in JSON evidence. This is not a complete explanation method \cite{doshi2017interpretable}, but it exceeds a single opaque score. GNNExplainer \cite{ying2019gnnexplainer} remains future work.

\section{What Results Prove and Do Not Prove}

\textbf{Proven on current artefacts:}
\begin{itemize}
\item Volatility artefacts can be converted into typed graphs from 86 to 27,362 nodes (descriptive corpus).
\item Rule-based triage produces HIGH/CRITICAL verdicts on most benign-labelled rows: 15/15 uncertain on \texttt{extracted\_data}; 22/22 uncertain on \texttt{extracted\_csvs}.
\item Manifest governance (\texttt{benign\_subtype}, \texttt{train\_eligible}) supports reproducible, family-aware evaluation design.
\item Evidence JSON and uncertainty fields are implementable alongside GNN scores.
\item Track~2/3 inference runs complete on $n{=}43$ and expose saturation and review-routing behaviour honestly.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Not proven:}
\begin{itemize}
\item Superior detection accuracy versus production AV or EDR on enterprise hosts.
\item Generalisation to unseen malware families without grouped CV on larger $n$.
\item That GNN structure beats strong tabular baselines on the same manifest (tabular CV is reference-only; Table~\ref{tab:tabular_baselines}).
\item Robustness to adversarial graph manipulation \cite{apruzzese2023sok}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item Small corpora: $n{=}30$ descriptive, $n{=}43$ ML---high metric variance under grouped CV.
\item Noisy benign labels: 15/15 uncertain on descriptive corpus; all 22 benign rows uncertain on ML corpus (\texttt{ambiguous\_novirus\_control}).
\item No \texttt{clean\_benign} manifold for strict benign one-class training at dissertation writing time.
\item Binary decisive accuracy against folder labels ${\approx}49\%$ when all rows are classified \texttt{likely\_malicious}; two-model decisive coverage ${\approx}0$.
\item Family-level pairing and numeric host IDs limit independent held-out family tests.
\item Capture timing and Windows build affect plugin output.
\item Heuristic graph attributes couple rules and learning.
\item Track~1 grouped CV was exploratory only; no backbone ranking is reported.
\end{itemize}

Sommer and Paxson \cite{sommer2010outside} and Sculley et al.\ \cite{sculley2015debt} apply: evaluation design and pipeline debt can dominate headline metrics.

\section{Practical Value}

The pipeline supports evidence-led triage \cite{case2017memory}: organise plugin output, surface rule verdicts, attach GNN scores as a secondary signal, and export JSON for analyst review. The uncertain-benign finding is operationally relevant---teams should expect HIGH verdicts on baseline images without treating them automatically as labelling errors.

\section{Summary}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Benign-labelled rows frequently exhibit HIGH/CRITICAL triage signals (15/15 uncertain on \texttt{extracted\_data}; 22/22 on \texttt{extracted\_csvs})---the central empirical finding.
\item Graph construction scales from tiny to very large memory snapshots.
\item Rule-based triage alone cannot define benign status from folder names.
\item Binary and two-model inference (Tracks~2/3) on $n{=}43$ show calibration, saturation, and analyst-review routing; Track~1 informed engineering but is not reported numerically.
\item Structured evidence improves usefulness over a single probability.
\end{enumerate}
